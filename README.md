# Brimholt

An original Godot tile-world prototype for up to **twelve LAN players**, including a
playing host. It uses native ENet, server-authoritative grid movement, collision,
smooth visual interpolation, a flat backpack with ground items, layered visible
equipment, and a 256×256 world with ferry travel. The map and all sprites are original;
the pixel art is generated by `tools/make_sprites.py` and the world is painted in Godot's
TileMap editor. Brimholt is a working name;
trademark/name availability has not been verified.

## Setup and LAN play

Requires **Godot 4.5+**; development checks use **4.5.1**. Open `project.godot` in
Godot and press **F5**, or run from this folder:

```sh
godot --path .
```

Replace `godot` with your executable path if it is not on PATH. There is no Node
server, external service, API key, or bundled engine dependency.

After a fresh checkout (or new sprite sheets), import the assets once before running
from the command line: open the project in the Godot editor, or run
`godot --headless --path . --editor --quit`. Otherwise sprites fail to load.

Without `--host` or `--join` (for example when you press F5 in the editor), the game opens
on a start menu over Cinder Crossing: pick a vocation and click **Host a game** in one
instance. In another instance, enter the host's LAN IPv4 address and click **Join**. Use `127.0.0.1` only for two instances on the same
computer. All computers must use the same project files. Allow inbound **UDP
1234** through the host firewall; ENet does not use TCP/WebSockets.

```sh
# Playing host
godot --path . -- --host

# Client on the same computer; replace the IP for another LAN computer
godot --path . -- --join=127.0.0.1

# Optional dedicated host: no host character, up to twelve clients
godot --headless --path . -- --headless-server
```

For an alternative UDP port, add `--port=1235` after `--` on every instance.
The world resets when the host stops; there is no persistence. This is a
**trusted-LAN prototype**, not an Internet-ready service. Do not expose its port
to the public Internet.

## Controls

These are the default keys; every key can be changed (see below).

| Control | Action |
| --- | --- |
| WASD / arrows | Move one cardinal tile; hold to repeat |
| Left-click a creature | Lock it as your target (click again to release) |
| Space | Target the nearest monster |
| 1–9 | Cast the spell in action bar slot 1–9 (filled with your vocation's spells) |
| Drag an item | Move it between backpack slots, equipment slots, and nearby tiles |
| Right-click item | Equip from the backpack, or unequip into the backpack |
| Right-click tile / G | Pick up the top item on a tile (G: your tile, else the one you face) |
| Hover an item | Show its slot and stats |
| Right-click a person | Open their menu (ferrymen: **Travel**) |
| Right-click / click a bar slot | Change what the slot does / use it |
| M | Open or close the world map |
| Esc | Close menus, or release your target (cannot be rebound) |
| Leave / close window | Disconnect; leaving the host ends the session |

Walls, water, trees, ferrymen, and players block movement. A gold ring marks your
character; the minimap in the side panel follows you.

### Action bar and key bindings

The nine-slot action bar sits at the bottom of the game window, with each slot's key in its
corner. The bar holds spells only: right-click a slot to pick one of your vocation's spells
or clear it. Change gear on the equipment panel; targeting, pick up, and the world map have
their own keys. Click **Controls** in the top bar to rebind any action: click a key button, then
press the new key. Each action has a primary and a secondary key; Backspace clears one,
Esc cancels, and a key already in use moves to the new action. Keys follow their physical
position, so WASD stays under your hand on any keyboard layout. Bindings and bar contents
are saved per computer in Godot's user data folder (`user://keybinds.cfg`).

### Combat

Combat follows Tibia's model. Left-click a monster (or Space for the nearest) to lock it
with a red square; you then attack automatically every **2 seconds** while it is in reach.
Knights fight in melee (any of the 8 surrounding tiles); Paladins (6 tiles), Sorcerers, and
Druids (5 tiles) attack at range, which needs a clear line of sight: walls and trees block
it, water does not. If your target is out of reach and you are not steering, you walk
toward it. Spells sit on the action bar; each costs mana, has its own cooldown, and starts
a shared cooldown for its group (attack 2 s, healing 1 s). Every vocation has Light heal;
Knights add Brutal blow and Whirling strike (level 10), Paladins Piercing shot and Holy
mend (level 8), Sorcerers Ember bolt and Flame wave (level 10), and Druids Frost bolt,
Mend, and Mass mend (level 10), which heals everyone within 3 tiles.

Monsters stand idle until a player comes within 6 tiles, then chase and attack, but never
farther than 12 tiles from their spawn or across a zone edge; out of reach, they walk home
and heal. They respawn 45 seconds after death, once no player stands within 4 tiles of the
spawn. Monsters chase along real paths around trees and walls. Level 1–4 voles and toads
ring Cinder Crossing; beyond them, levels rise to 35 in the far east and south, and
Blackreed Isle holds levels 40–50. Spawns sit in small clearings at least 7 steps from
roads, so travel between towns stays safe. Early monsters hit softer (half damage at level
1, full at level 11), and a monster's name shows its level in white (fair), yellow (tough),
or red (5+ levels above you). Experience is shared by damage dealt; level 2 takes about 4
kills. Monsters drop loot from their own table (see below), spilling onto nearby tiles when
the corpse tile is full. Health and mana regenerate every 2 seconds,
three times faster after 8 seconds out of combat. Players can only attack each other while both stand in a PvP zone (Blackreed
Isle); leaving it drops the target.

### Parties and shared experience

Right-click another player to **Invite to party**; they right-click you to **Join party**
(or you **Withdraw invitation**). Right-click yourself or a member to **Leave party**. Only
the leader invites; when the leader leaves, the member who joined the game first leads, and
a party of one disbands. Disconnecting leaves the party. Nameplates show a blue shield for
members (gold rim for the leader) and a faint shield for open invitations. Party members
cannot attack each other, even on Blackreed Isle.

Like Tibia's shared experience, the party pools its members' share of each kill and splits
it evenly among members who are within 20 tiles of the kill and fought, cast a spell
(healing too), or were hit in the last 2 minutes. So a Knight who tanks while a Sorcerer
deals the damage levels just as fast. Sharing needs at least two such members whose lowest
level is at least two thirds of the highest (otherwise everyone keeps their own damage
share). Distinct vocations among them multiply the pool: ×1.2 for one, ×1.3 for two, ×1.6
for three, and ×2 for all four.

### Loot

Each monster has a loot table in `game/monsters.gd` (`Monsters.LOOT`): every `[item,
chance]` row rolls independently, then gold piles are added (one per 10 levels plus a 60%
chance of one more; elites +3). Rows are a material unique to the monster (vole tails, toad
slime, boar tusks, bandit badges, wolf pelts, ... warden crests; 20–50% chance) and
occasional equipment (1–8%). Materials have a shop `value` in `World.ITEMS` for the
upcoming shops. Add a monster's row to `LOOT`, and a new material to `World.ITEMS`,
`SpriteArt.ITEM_ORDER`, and `tools/make_sprites.py` (icon shape and palette). Loot is not
reserved for the killer yet.

### Death and skulls

Dying costs 10% of your total experience (which can lose levels) and returns you to Cinder
Crossing at full health. **Below level 8 you keep your items and gold**, so an early death
cannot strand you. From level 8, as in classic Tibia without blessings, you always lose your whole
backpack and all purse gold, and each worn item has a 10% chance to drop. Everything lost
lands on the tile where you fell, spilling onto tiles within 3 steps (six items per tile),
and anyone can pick it up, including you on a run back to your body.

Attacking an unmarked player gives you a **white skull** for 15 minutes (renewed while you
keep attacking). Fighting back against someone who attacked you, or attacking a skulled
player, is self-defence and gives no skull. Killing an unmarked player who did not attack
you is an **unjustified kill**; three within 24 hours give a **red skull** for 30 days, and a
red-skulled player who dies loses everything they wear as well. Skulls show next to the
name above the character and stay after death. Yellow and black skulls are not used.

On screen, creatures show a name and a health bar that turns yellow and red, damage rises
in red (critical hits in orange), healing in green, and experience in white. Ranged attacks
fly as projectiles and area spells flash their tiles. Your health and mana bars sit in the
top-left of the game window.

### Talking to people

There is no typed dialogue: right-click a person to open their menu and pick an option.
Ferrymen offer **Travel**; shops (**Buy**) and **Quests** will use the same menu later.

## The world and ferries

The world is 256×256 tiles: walking straight across takes about 46 seconds. Cinder
Crossing, the starting town, sits in the north-west corner with gates to the east and
south. A sea runs down the whole western edge with four port towns on its coast:
**Cinder Landing**, **Mossford**, **Saltmere**, and **Wrenhollow**. Their ferries sail
between each other. Inland to the east lies one large, irregular lake with the town of
**Reedmere** on its west shore. Its ferry is the only way to **Blackreed Isle**, the
island in the lake: a **level 40–50 PvP hunting ground**. Entering it shows a red banner,
and the island is tinted red on the minimap and world map.

Each port has a ferryman at the end of its pier. Stand within 2 tiles, right-click him,
choose **Travel**, and pick a destination; the host checks your
distance, the route, that you are standing still, a 1-second travel cooldown, and your
gold, then deducts the fare and lands you next to the destination ferryman. A crowded
dock refuses passengers.

Everyone starts with 100 gold. Gold coin piles lie around the wilds; picking one up adds
25 gold to your purse without using a backpack slot. Coastal fares are 15–30 gold; the
island crossing costs 40 each way. Extra gold lies near the island's monster spawns.

## Designing the world

The world is the scene `game/maps/overworld.tscn`; open it in the Godot editor:

1. **Terrain**: select the `Terrain` node and use the TileMap panel at the bottom to paint
   with the palette (`game/maps/terrain.tres`). Each tile's custom data `terrain` sets its
   gameplay: grass `.`, dirt road `,`, water `~`, stone wall `#`, tree `T`, sand `:`,
   cobblestone `=`, pier/bridge `b`. Water, walls, and trees block movement. The editor
   shows flat palette tiles; in game they become oblique walls, tall trees, shorelines, and
   plank piers. Keep the map at non-negative coordinates; unpainted cells are void.
2. **Spawns**: the twelve `Marker2D` nodes under `Spawns`, one per player, in order.
3. **Ferrymen**: nodes under `Ferrymen`. The node name is the ferryman's id; set the name,
   dock name, facing, and `routes` (destination ferryman id → fare in gold) in the
   Inspector. Put a ferryman at the end of a pier with walkable tiles within 2 tiles.
4. **Items**: nodes under `Items` with an item id from `game/world.gd`, such as
   `gold_coins` or `glowcap`. Several markers on one tile make a stack.
5. **Zones**: nodes under `Zones`, placed on a tile corner, with a size in tiles, a name,
   a PvP flag, and a level range. The editor shows them as red rectangles.
6. **Monster spawns**: nodes under `MonsterSpawns` with a monster id from
   `game/monsters.gd`, level (1–50), count, and spawn radius.

A marker belongs to the tile its position falls in. Save the scene and run the rules
check: it verifies terrain, markers, routes, zone and spawn levels, and that every
ferryman and item can be reached on foot or by boat from the spawns. Every player must use the same map, because
it is part of the connection handshake. `tools/make_map.gd` generated the first draft;
rerunning it with `godot --headless --path . --script res://tools/make_map.gd -- --force`
**replaces your edits**, so treat the scene as the source of truth.

## Presentation

The game window shows 15×11 tiles at 2× zoom and follows your character smoothly,
Tibia-style; the dedicated-server window centres the whole map. Walls are oblique:
their tops are raised up and to the left, and the world is drawn row by row so
walls nearer the camera (south/east) cover characters and items behind them.
Characters stand about 1.3 tiles tall with adult proportions, a 3/4 view, a
cloak, hood, and tabard tinted per player, dithered shading, and a two-step walk.
Grass, dirt, sand, cobblestone, animated water, plank piers, stone walls, trees, worn
gear, and item icons are sprites too. Trees stand about two tiles tall and hide whatever
walks behind them. Only the visible part of the map is drawn each frame.

## Art and sprites

`assets/sprites` holds original PNG sprite sheets made by `tools/make_sprites.py`
(Python standard library only). `game/sprite_art.gd` draws them with nearest
filtering:

| Sheet | Layout |
| --- | --- |
| `body.png`, `cloth.png`, `marker.png`, `gear/<item>.png` | 64×64 frames; columns stand / step A / step B; rows down / up / left / right; tile centre at frame pixel (32, 40) |
| `items.png` | 32×32 icons in `SpriteArt.ITEM_ORDER` |
| `tiles.png` | 32×32 cells: 4 grass, 4 dirt, 4 water frames, dirt edges and shorelines (N, E, S, W), 4 sand, 4 cobblestone, 2 horizontal and 2 vertical plank tiles |
| `walls.png` | 48×48 oblique walls drawn 16 px up-left of their tile; index = open sides (1 N, 2 W, 4 S, 8 E) |
| `objects.png` | 64×96 trees; trunk base on the tile centre at cell pixel (32, 80) |
| `terrain_palette.png` | 32×32 map-editor palette, one tile per terrain |

A character is drawn as: worn hand item when facing up, body, `cloth.png` tinted
with the player colour (it is greyscale), armour, helmet, then the hand item when
not facing up. Gear sheets already leave holes where the hood or near arm must stay
visible, so each item needs only one sheet rather than one per combination.

To edit art, change a PNG in Aseprite or another pixel editor, keeping its frame
layout, then reimport (open the editor or run the import command above). Running
`python3 tools/make_sprites.py` regenerates **all** sheets and overwrites hand edits;
add `--preview .tools/sprite-preview.png` for a 2× review sheet. A new item needs a
catalog entry in `game/world.gd`, an icon, and (if equippable) a gear sheet listed in
`game/sprite_art.gd`; the rules check verifies these exist.

## Inventory

The right-hand panel shows a Tibia-style paperdoll with eight slots (necklace,
helmet, charm / main hand, armor, off hand / two rings), an eight-slot backpack, and
your stat sheet. Everyone starts with an Ember cap, Moss coat, Copper blade, Oak
buckler, and Copper band. A Garnet ring, River pendant, Hearth charm, and a few other
items lie around Cinder Crossing. Rings fit either ring slot; right-clicking a ring
fills an empty ring slot first. Helmets, armor, and both hands are drawn on the
character; rings, necklaces, and charms are too small to show. Items can be dropped
on any walkable tile, stacking up to six per tile, and the top item is picked up.
Ground tiles must be your own tile or one of the eight around you.

Peer 1 validates every item move: location shape, reach, slot type, free space,
stack limit, and a 150 ms item cooldown. Dragging onto an occupied slot swaps
items when both fit; a worn item displaced from the ground goes back to that tile.
Equipped items are visible to everyone; backpack contents are sent only to their
owner. Late joiners receive ground items in the snapshot. Items carried by a
player who leaves disappear with them; ground items remain until the host stops.
The backpack is flat: there are no nested containers or trading.

## Stats and balance

Pick a vocation in the top bar before hosting or joining (or pass `--vocation=knight`,
`paladin`, `sorcerer`, or `druid` after `--`). The four follow the classic roles:
**Knight** (tank: most health, base defense), **Paladin** (ranged damage, light healing),
**Sorcerer** (burst damage, frail), and **Druid** (healer, frail). The host validates the
choice during the handshake; everyone sees each player's vocation and level.

Each item has stats (health, mana, attack, defense, critical chance, regeneration,
healing) that add to your vocation's base stats for your level; the stat sheet shows the
totals and the gear bonus. Charms, rings, and necklaces give passive stats only. Stats are
computed with the same shared function on every peer from server-authoritative vocation,
level, and equipment.

`game/balance.gd` defines the level 1-50 curves, all derived from a **350,000-health
final boss** that needs a raid of **at least ten level-50 players with every vocation**:
an enrage timer demands damage, raid-wide pressure demands healers, and wound stacks
demand two tanks. See [`docs/balance.md`](docs/balance.md) for the anchors, vocation
profiles, raid checks, and full level table.
Combat uses these stats and curves directly.

## Structure and checks

`game/world.gd` loads the map scene (terrain, spawns, items, ferrymen, zones, monster
spawns) and owns the item catalog and authoritative movement, inventory, and travel rules.
`game/combat.gd` runs targeting, attacks, spells, monster AI, deaths, experience, loot,
and respawns on the host; `game/monsters.gd` and `game/spells.gd` are their catalogs. `game/main.gd` handles ENet,
sender-validated RPCs, host/join UI, the camera, the inventory panel,
drag-and-drop, the ferry menu, the minimap and world map, and interpolation.
`game/world_view.gd` draws the visible, depth-ordered part of the world and `game/sprite_art.gd` maps tiles, walls, characters, gear, and icons to
sprite-sheet regions. Peer 1 keeps gameplay authority; clients request actions,
never positions, item contents, or appearance.
The connection handshake checks matching map scene, catalog, ground items, inventory
and movement parameters, player capacity, and protocol. Command-line startup exits nonzero if hosting
cannot bind its UDP port or the supplied join address is empty.

From this folder, with Godot 4.5+ and Python 3 (standard library only):

```sh
godot --headless --path . --editor --quit
godot --headless --path . --script res://tests/rules_test.gd
python3 tools/test_lan.py --godot godot
python3 tools/test_lan.py --godot godot --dedicated
python3 tools/test_lan.py --godot godot --host-loss
python3 tools/test_lan.py --godot godot --occupied-port

# Graphical checks, rendered offscreen; writes .tools/visual-report.html
python3 tools/test_visual.py --godot godot

# Automated full-game playthrough (about 5 minutes); writes .tools/playtest/report.html
python3 tools/playtest.py --godot godot
```

The rules checks cover movement, reservations, collision, spawns, equipment slots and
ring swaps, item stats, the balance anchors and curves, sprite sheet layout and coverage,
and inventory moves (malformed locations, reach, slot types, swaps, full backpack,
stack limit, cooldown boundary, and backpack privacy), the map scene (known terrain,
spawn/item/ferryman markers, routes, and reachability of every ferryman and item on foot
or by boat), ferry travel (range, routes, malformed requests, standing still, fares,
cooldown, crowded docks), gold pickup, zones and monster spawns (levels within 1–50 and
their zone, Blackreed Isle as a level 40–50 PvP zone reached only from Reedmere), and key
bindings (defaults 1–9, rebinding moves conflicting keys, Escape is reserved, save/load,
and malformed or duplicate saved values), and combat: catalogs and spawns, line of sight
and reach, target validation (malformed, unknown, self, too far, PvP only in PvP zones),
the two-second attack boundary and damage range, monster aggro, chasing onto free tiles
only, leashing home and healing, experience shared by damage and level ups, loot tables
(every monster has one, catalog items with icons and shop values, seeded determinism,
drop rates, elite gold, spilling past a full corpse tile), parties (invites, joining,
leadership hand-over, disbanding, no friendly fire, shared experience with the vocation
bonus, activity, range and level-ratio rules, disconnect clean-up),
respawn delays, the 10% death penalty and town respawn, spells (vocation, level, target,
mana, cooldowns and groups, heal caps, area hits, mass heal radius), regeneration, item
loss (backpack and purse always, worn items about 10%, everything with a red skull, spill
within the stack limit), and skulls (white for unprovoked attacks, self-defence, renewal,
unjustified kills, the third giving a red skull, the 24-hour window, expiry). The bounded ENet harness
runs a real playing host, client, and late joiner over loopback, checking
authority, cooldowns, collision, equipment updates, rejected item moves, a drop
seen in the late-join ground snapshot and then picked up by the late joiner,
a party invitation accepted through the right-click options (private invitations, public
membership) and disbanded when the member disconnects, backpack privacy, snapshots, and
disconnect cleanup. `--dedicated` checks a server
without a playing character and a moving, equipping, dropping, picking-up, and
ring-equipping client, checks the replicated level and backpack privacy, and sails the
client from Cinder Landing to Mossford after rejected out-of-range, unknown-route, and
malformed travel requests. (The server test script carries the client to the dock
instead of walking.) Then the server places a weak monster beside the client, which sends
a malformed target, locks the monster, casts Piercing shot, and must see floating damage,
the kill, its experience, and its cleared target; the late joiner must receive every
monster in its snapshot. `--host-loss` checks the exact disconnect error and
clearing stale client state. `--occupied-port` checks an explicit hosting error
and exit status 1 while another socket holds the port.

Each peer has a 20-second deadline; multiplayer scenarios have a 30-second outer
deadline. The occupied-port check has a 10-second deadline. The harness stops only
its own processes and fails on unexpected Godot errors; only the exact expected
disconnect message is accepted in the host-loss scenario. Inspect headless editor
output for script/resource errors, not just its exit status.

The graphical check renders the game into an offscreen buffer; Godot only opens a
64×64 borderless window in the top-left corner, so nothing flashes over your work; it
ignores OS close requests. It writes
ignored captures (equipped, unequipped, forest, Cinder Landing, world map) and
`.tools/visual-report.html` (open it in a browser or an editor's browser panel). It checks pixel changes in each character's region
and each equipment panel slot after removing gear, four facings, ground item and
raised oblique wall pixels matching their sprite texels, the camera staying centred and gliding with the
character, an interpolated movement midpoint/endpoint, a tree sprite in depth order,
sailing through the real right-click menu and route list (Cinder Landing to Mossford,
then Reedmere to Blackreed Isle), the island's PvP banner, casting the spell in action
bar slot 1 with its key (the bar holds spells only), and opening and closing the world map with its bound key.
Key presses use the player's current bindings. Finally it spawns two monsters, locks one
with a real left-click, and checks the red target square and a landed hit. The map's own
monsters are removed at the start so the pixel checks stay deterministic. It also uses simulated mouse
input sent to the offscreen buffer to start a drag from the backpack, right-click a
tile to pick up, and right-click a backpack item to equip; the drop itself is delivered
to the map's drop handler.
Its 20-second timeout stays active through the assertions.

The playthrough starts a real dedicated server and two bot clients (`tests/playthrough.gd`),
a Knight and a Sorcerer, that play the game through the same calls as the UI: they form
a party, equip the start kit, walk out of town with pathfinding, hunt the nearest monsters
with spells and healing (checking that party sharing keeps the Knight level with the
Sorcerer), pick up gold and nearby monster materials, finish their fight, walk to Cinder Landing and sail to Mossford, walk to Reedmere
(routing around monster spawns), and sail to Blackreed Isle. There the Knight leaves the
party and attacks the
Sorcerer (white skull), the Sorcerer fights back (no skull), and the Knight kills him
(unjustified kill), then loots the dropped items while the Sorcerer checks his empty
backpack and purse in town. A bot that dies earlier walks back to recover its belongings (or, under beginner
protection, carries on).
The report lists each step as ok, fail, or finding (gameplay observations such as slow
levelling) with screenshots. Bots render offscreen in two borderless 64×64 windows in the
top-left corner. macOS may still give them keyboard focus, so test and bot windows
ignore OS close requests; the orchestrator stops them when the run ends. Headless checks do
**not** establish visual quality. Inspect the images or play with two windows to
assess it. Networking scenarios run on local loopback; **separate physical-machine
LAN play remains unverified** and still needs network/firewall checks.

## Scope and reference licensing

Blessings, yellow and black skulls, loot protection, the final raid boss, nested containers, trading, shops, quests, procedural caves, floors, NPCs other
than the menu-driven ferrymen, persistence, and authentication are outside this prototype. Vocations and stats exist but are not yet
used in play.

[The Forgotten Server](https://github.com/otland/forgottenserver) (GPL-2.0) and
[OTClient Redemption](https://github.com/mehah/otclient) (MIT) are useful references
for high-level container/item concepts and inventory-event/UI separation, not
Godot/ENet dependencies. No third-party code or assets are imported here. Do not
copy or translate GPL code into this project without a licensing decision.
MIT reuse requires checking the specific files/dependencies and retaining their
copyright/license notices. Neither engine's source license grants rights to
proprietary Tibia sprites, maps, or client data.
